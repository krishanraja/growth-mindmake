# Evidence register: who decides

Checked 17 November 2026. The proof behind the thesis "AI makes strong judgement stronger and weak judgement weaker. Mindmake builds the strong kind." (Ruling, Krish, 2026-11-17; docs/RULINGS.md).

Rules for using it:
- Quote a figure only with its source in the same sentence, and keep its limit in mind.
- Re-check a figure before reusing it after 30 days, and update the date here.
- Never write that institutional brains have replaced leaders, that AI damages thinking, or that executives specifically fall for wrong AI advice: no study shows those yet. Say the direction of travel, or say it is our reading.
- An item marked "verify first" is not quoted until it is checked against its own publisher.

## The thesis itself

| ID | Finding | Source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Across 106 experiments, human and AI combinations beat humans alone on average, but gained where the human was better than the AI at the task and lost where the AI was better (effect sizes 0.46 and -0.54). | Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone, "When combinations of humans and AI are useful", Nature Human Behaviour, October 2024, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167/ | Lab tasks, studies from 2020 to mid-2023, very varied results |
| E2 | With a GPT-4 business adviser, high-performing entrepreneurs gained about 15% and low performers did about 8% worse; no average effect. | Otis, Clarke, Delecourt, Holtz and Koning, Management Science, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909 | Kenyan small businesses; figures vary by paper version |

## People follow AI even when it is wrong

| ID | Finding | Source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| E3 | On a task outside AI's capability, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce a correct solution; inside it they did 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with over 40% higher quality. | Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier", Harvard Business School working paper 24-013, 2023; 758 BCG consultants | One firm, GPT-4 era, one outside task |
| E4 | With deliberately wrong AI suggestions, very experienced radiologists' accuracy fell to 45.5%. | Dratsch et al., Radiology, May 2023; 27 radiologists | Small sample, simulated AI |
| E5 | "Higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking." | Lee et al., Carnegie Mellon and Microsoft Research, CHI 2025, https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778; 319 knowledge workers | Self-reported |

|---|---|---|---|

## What the evidence does not show

No study measures automation bias in senior executives or boards. Evidence that AI wears down thinking is thin (small, student or confounded studies). There is no measured displacement of leaders, and no aggregate sign of labour market disruption in the US so far (Yale Budget Lab, 2025 and 2026).
