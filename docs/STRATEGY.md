This page is the strategy on one page. It links the playbooks instead of repeating them. The number it serves is in docs/NORTH_STAR.md: good meetings a week, each marked good by Krish and traced to its path.

## The thesis

**AI makes strong judgement stronger and weak judgement weaker. Mindmake builds the strong kind** (Ruling, Krish, 2026-11-17). Every lane argues it. A leader who hands their judgement to AI may not notice: professionals follow AI even when it is wrong, and people who trust it more think less critically. A leader who keeps it gets faster and better. What a leader must keep: judgement, standards, reading the numbers, and directing AI to bring complex information together for clarity and speed of thought. The proof, with dates and limits, is in docs/EVIDENCE.md; nothing is quoted without its source.

The first lead magnet is **"Who decided?"**, a free 20-minute audit of one real recent decision made with AI's help. The second is Krish's own founding story, told under his name. Both carry one ask.

## From interest to a meeting

Interest is a reply, a free brief, or a kit bought by a senior buyer (that last trigger only on his yes). Then:

1. **Who is this?** Control Center resolves the person to one LinkedIn profile, where name, current company and role all agree. If any doubt remains it stops and asks Krish, with the candidates. It never guesses.
2. **A proposal that knows them.** What we read about the person and the company, each fact with its source; the three decisions likely costing them most, labelled as our reading; what an AI brain would do for each; a thirty-day proof shaped to their case, with no price.
3. **A PDF worth forwarding,** in Mindmake's own design, reaching them as a tracked per-person link.
4. **A reply in his voice,** in the same thread, with the link and his Calendly link, waiting in his Gmail for his review and his send.
5. **The meeting,** tapped good after it and traced to its path.

This runs in Control Center, which is private, because every step carries a named person. This public repository only counts.

## Parts of your AI brain

Seven parts, $59 each, one-off, for senior executives (Rulings, Krish, 2026-11-17). Each does one plain job a leader would do before a decision: Fact-check my board update; Fact-check an email before I send it; Check a number before I rely on it; Brief me before a meeting; Work out my lowest price; Test a discount before I agree to it; Audit a decision I made with AI. The one line that explains them all: "Your AI brain is your judgement, written down so AI can use it. Each part does one job you'd do before a decision, using your own files and your own rules, and then hands the decision back to you." A part is working software that plugs into Claude in one click, runs on the leader's own files with no Mindmake infrastructure, shows a result in five minutes and ends by naming what only the leader can judge. Six operational parts (ad spend, recurring payments, sales attribution, quoting, prospect ranking, deal revival) are built and held back: housekeeping, not judgement. Parts earn money now and are the smallest proof of the AI brain; the whole brain is the thirty-day proof. They sell at mindmake.co/parts with a 14-day refund and no scarcity. The software is in krishanraja/mindmake-kits; the page is in the mindmake repository.

## The arithmetic

To reach the ladder (docs/NORTH_STAR.md) the lanes need roughly 25 sends a day between his box and Jonny's, about one reply in ten, and about half of replies booking. These are working assumptions, not forecasts. The daily sends line and the weekly review show which stage is weakest within two days, and that stage gets the next change.
