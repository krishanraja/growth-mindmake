# Copy and offer doctrine

Apply the copywriting, offers and demand corpus to Mindmake's warm, consultative motion. The [preserved corpus](../../_corpus/copywriting-offers-demand-corpus.md) is reference material, archived byte for byte on 19 October 2026. This playbook is the engine's application of it. Where the two differ, this playbook and the repository rules win; the [override list](#where-this-engines-rules-change-the-corpus) names each difference.

It serves the North Star directly: leaders who arrive already asking for the brain because someone they trust told them are the corpus's "most aware" buyer, produced by warm paths and rooms rather than by lists.

## The buyer and the desire

The buyer is a founder, principal or commercial leader who owns an important decision and can expense the work. Nobody expenses fear. Frame the desire as the leader's own ambition: better judgement at scale, a commercial choice made with confidence, a system they own. From the corpus's motive map ("Cashvertising"), the honest motives here are status and competence, autonomy, and protection of the business. Never manufacture a threat, agitate pain or imply the reader is falling behind ("Fear versus ethics").

"Demand before prose" and "Reader before product" (Final Operating Doctrine 1 and 2) already match the engine: a verified buying moment, real relationship or expressed problem must exist before anything is drafted. Copy never rescues a missing trigger. If the brief is weak, return `blocked`, not a better sentence.

## Diagnose the weakest link first

The corpus's persuasion chain (Part I) maps onto evidence this engine already holds. Before rewriting a draft, name the weakest link and fix that one.

| Link | Buyer question | Mindmake evidence that answers it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance | Is this for me? | Buying moment with primary source, real relationship, role inference marked as inference |
| Attention | Why notice this? | Opening on what changed in their world, never on Mindmake |
| Desire | Do I want the result? | One decision the leader owns, in their words where authorised |
| Difference | Why this? | The capability description from canon, unchanged |
| Belief | Will it work? | Cleared proof with permission for this placement, or none |
| Value | Is it worth it? | The useful contribution itself: an aid, an answer, a sharper question |
| Safety | What if it fails? | Outside-in framing, a reversible small test, no pressure |
| Timing | Why now? | The observed trigger only. No invented deadline |
| Action | What exactly do I do? | One ask from the approved routes |

## Awareness, lead type and ask by path

Schwartz's awareness levels ("Breakthrough Advertising") and the six lead types ("Great Leads") depend on how the leader reached us. Label the awareness level in the private brief before drafting, and use the most direct lead the reader can accept ("Phase 7: Choose the lead").

| Path | Typical awareness | Lead type | Opening move | One ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction or referral from a trusted person | Solution or product aware: the connector has named the problem and Mindmake | Promise | The decision the connector recognised, then the canon description | A concrete reply or consultation step |
| Leader already asking for the brain | Most aware | Offer | The next step itself, plainly | The consultation step Krish chooses, in a personal exchange |
| Existing relationship | Problem aware | Problem-Solution or Story | The real last touch and the decision now in front of them | Permission to share a specific aid |
| Room: talk, host pitch, selected post | Unaware to problem aware | Story or Proclamation | A supported shift the audience can act on | None, or one supported route |
| Named cold | Unaware to problem aware | Story built on the observed trigger | What changed, with outside-in framing | The free AI brief or permission to share an aid |
| Inbound request or reply | Solution aware | Promise | The leader's own words, summarised faithfully | One missing fact or the next agreed step |

The connector's forwardable explanation in [introductions](01-network-and-introductions.md) is its own asset: its reader is the connector, its awareness is low, and its ask is permission only.

Treat AI as a sophisticated market ("Sophistication"). Generic AI promises are exhausted, so louder claims fail. Identify with the leader's actual decision and let the canon's mechanism, a system holding their own standards and judgement, carry the difference.

## Rule of One for every note

Each draft has one reader, one decision as its big idea, one emotion (confidence or ambition, never fear), one promise, one supporting fact or story with its evidence ID, and one ask ("The Rule of One"). Supporting points may be several; they all serve the one decision. A note that could move unchanged to another recipient fails the Rule of One and the [outreach rejection test](03-named-prospect-outreach.md#reject-the-draft-when).

For a cold first contact, use the corpus's direct outreach structure: relevant observation, concise outcome hypothesis, credible reason for contact, one low-friction next step, and an easy correction. Personalisation changes the relevance of the message, never just a token.

## Difference: USP and big idea from canon only

Use Reeves's form ("The USP test") as a private check, not recipient copy:

> For [leader in this decision], [capability] provides [canon description] because [cleared reason to believe].

The two capabilities and their reviewed descriptions are fixed by the canon pinned in the frozen growth contract:

- **Build your AI brain:** "Your taste, standards and judgement, running as a system you own."
- **Build your AI revenue engine:** "Your pricing, positioning and team rebuilt for AI, then tested with real buyers."

Select the capability from the decision in front of the person. The "reason to believe" must be an evidence ID or cleared proof card, or the sentence goes. Never write a new offer fact, outcome, timeframe, guarantee or price. A sharper USP or big idea is a strategy proposal for Krish with its rationale, never a draft change.

## Feature, benefit, proof

The corpus's feature-benefit-proof matrix ("The Copywriter's Handbook" and Part V) maps onto the `claims` array each prompt already returns. Every material claim names its classification (`observed_fact`, `supported_inference`, `unknown`), its evidence IDs and, for proof, its `permission_ref`. Ask "so what?" until the claim reaches something the leader values, then reverse-check that the evidence supports that exact wording.

## Proof hierarchy and proof cards

Map the corpus's proof hierarchy ("Phase 6") onto proof permissions:

1. Direct outcome, demonstration or comparable case: only cleared client proof, in its proof family, with permission for this placement.
2. Process proof and mechanism: the canon descriptions and a cleared operating artifact.
3. Credentials: only cleared ones, relevant to the decision.
4. Testimonials: exact approved wording, within proof family and placement scope.
5. Logical argument: outside-in reasoning, labelled as an illustrative view to test against the real business.
6. Unsupported assertion: blocked.

Attendance proves attendance, never client status. Research supports commentary, never "what we saw with a client". The corpus's proof card fields map to this engine as: `claim_id` to evidence ID, `source` to the authorised source reference, `limitations` to the claim's limitation, `approved_wording` to the exact permitted quotation, `expiry_or_review_date` to canon and evidence freshness, and placement to the permission scope (for speaking, the `proof` object's `cleared` and `placement`). Place proof next to the claim it supports ("Influence": make proof proximate).

## Ethical influence

Use one Cialdini principle that resolves the live doubt; never stack them ("Influence"). Reciprocity is a decision aid useful without any purchase. Authority is cleared credentials only. Social proof is cleared, comparable proof only. Commitment is a next step the leader stated. Liking is respect, not flattery. Unity is a genuine shared room or membership. Scarcity is never used: capacity is private.

## Value Equation as a design lens for proof scopes

Hormozi's Value Equation ("$100M Offers") is a lens for proposals to Krish about a paid proof, not engine authority over offers, pricing or guarantees. Map it to the proof scope in [inbound, qualification and proof](06-inbound-qualification-and-proof.md#consult-and-scope):

| Lever | Proof scope element |
| --- | --- |
| Dream outcome | One decision or capability and the result it should improve, in the leader's words |
| Likelihood | Observable acceptance criteria, who judges them, comparable cleared proof, and the system the client keeps |
| Time delay | A working first version used on real work |
| Effort and sacrifice | Named owner, bounded inputs and data access, explicit exclusions |

The bonus, guarantee, urgency and pricing steps of "Offer construction" are Krish's and the canon's. If the lens suggests one, raise it as a strategy proposal with its reasoning.

## Browne's five-step sale in qualification

The corpus's consultative sale ("The Secret of Selling Anything") fits the [qualification prompt](../../prompts/qualification.md) and the lifecycle stage. The engine prepares; Krish holds the conversation; human confirmation owns qualification. Preparation changes no outcome.

| Step | Qualification evidence it produces |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Identify motivation | `owned_problem`, `active_need` |
| 2. Summarise and confirm | The decision restated and checked with the leader |
| 3. Present selectively | `offer_fit`: the one capability that serves the stated decision |
| 4. Answer questions | Objections recorded as information, with the matrix below |
| 5. Close | `agreed_next_step` with owner and timing; `decision_authority` from evidence, never from title |

Prepare a short discovery list for Krish from the "Sales discovery guide": what are you trying to make happen; what made this a priority now; what has the current approach prevented; what have you tried; what must a solution preserve or avoid; how will the decision be made and who else is affected; what would make the result credible; may I summarise what I heard. Honest non-fit is a good outcome: route it to `useful_answer`, `refer_with_permission` or `close`.

## Objection matrix

| Objection | Underlying doubt | Prepared response | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Will it work for us? | Context transfer | The mechanism in canon terms and a bounded proof scope | Comparable cleared proof, or say none exists |
| We do not have time | Effort | Named inputs, exclusions and the first working version | The scope itself |
| Too expensive | Value | Krish discusses fee privately; the draft scopes the decision and result | None in a draft; no figures |
| Not now | Timing | Acknowledge, leave a useful aid, record any revisit condition | The real trigger, or none |
| Need approval | Multi-stakeholder | A forwardable decision aid | Sourced aid |
| We already use AI tools | Sophistication | The difference between tools and a system holding their judgement | Canon description |

Training-only, certification-only or task automation requests route elsewhere rather than stretching the promise.

## Core Four mapped to permitted motions

| Corpus motion ("$100M Leads") | Mindmake motion |
| --- | --- |
| Warm one-to-one | Existing relationships and follow-up on actual replies |
| Warm one-to-many | Selected founder posts to Krish's own audience |
| Cold one-to-one | Researched named cold only: one person, one live decision |
| Cold one-to-many | Rooms: talks, host pitches, reusable decision aids |
| Others | Introductions and referrals with two opt-ins; a future selling partner |
| Paid media | Excluded |

The free AI brief and the decision aids are the existing lead magnets in the corpus's sense: they diagnose, help on their own and bridge to the paid proof. Mindmake owns the brief. No lists, no paid acquisition, no generic sequences.

## Money model as framing only

"$100M Money Models" frames the path: the free brief and consultation attract, the paid proof is the core, and continuity is a working system the leader keeps improving. This framing explains why a paid proof must leave the leader a system they keep. The engine designs no upsell, downsell or continuity offer. A referral prompt comes only after realised value, with separate permission and proof rights.

## Test record mapped to the weekly experiment

The corpus's test record ("Part V") maps onto the [weekly review's](../../prompts/weekly-review.md) `experiment_proposal`: `decision_question` to `next_human_decision`, `hypothesis` to `hypothesis`, control and challenger to `comparison` and `one_change`, `primary_metric` to `success_signal`, guardrails to `adverse_signal`, end date to `review_trigger`. Test in the corpus's priority order, within the engine's scope: promise and angle of a decision aid, lead type, proof placement, then ask. Market and offer tests are Krish's. At this volume, results are directional; keep the control and record context, not only winners.

## Before drafting

- Is there an observed trigger, real relationship or expressed request, with evidence IDs?
- Which path, awareness level and lead type? Recorded privately.
- Which one decision, capability and canon description?
- Which proof, if any, is cleared for this placement?
- Which single ask fits the path?
- Is anything missing? Then `blocked` or `needs_review`, not fluent copy over the gap.

## Before `ready_for_review`: edit passes A to E

Run the corpus's edit passes ("Phase 9") in order and diagnose before rewriting ("Critique instruction"):

- **A, strategy:** one reader, one awareness level, one decision, one promise, one ask.
- **B, evidence:** every material claim resolves to an evidence ID; proof is proximal and in scope; limitations visible.
- **C, flow:** each paragraph earns the next; objections answered where they arise.
- **D, clarity:** British English, familiar words, short paragraphs, no jargon, no em dashes.
- **E, action:** the ask is exact, the reason now is genuine, response friction is low.

Separate evidence problems from prose problems. Do not polish style while relevance, promise or proof are incoherent.

## Rubric for drafts

Score each dimension 0 to 4 ("Part VI"). Record the total and the weakest link in the result's existing `limitations` or review notes, outside the recipient-facing body; no new JSON field.

| Dimension | 4 means |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | One named reader and a sourced trigger |
| Desire | One decision the leader owns, framed as ambition |
| Awareness fit | Lead and depth match the path |
| Differentiation | Canon description, unchanged and relevant |
| Contribution | Useful without a purchase |
| Proof | Cleared, proximal, in scope, or honestly absent |
| Clarity | Immediate and concrete |
| Flow | Each paragraph earns the next |
| Objections | The likely doubt answered where it arises |
| Ethics | No invented urgency, familiarity or fear; reversible |
| Action | One exact, low-friction ask |
| Evidence trail | Every claim resolves; closure condition named |

Below 28 of 48, return `needs_review` with the weakest link named. A high score makes a draft worth Krish's review; it proves nothing about outcomes.

## Where this engine's rules change the corpus

1. Never a list. The corpus's list and market primacy becomes warm paths, rooms and researched named individuals; bought, rented or scraped lists are excluded.
2. One ask per message, from the approved routes. No follow-up sequence and no "build the follow-up before launch".
3. No invented urgency, scarcity, deadline, guarantee, testimonial, statistic or familiarity. The corpus's "real" urgency is used only when an observed source supplies it.
4. Approved testimonials are quoted exactly, within their proof family and placement scope; attendance is never client proof.
5. Private fees, capacity, client records and buyer psychology are never reproduced. Price and terms are discussed by Krish in person.
6. Agents prepare and Krish acts. Nothing is sent, published, scheduled or spent; the corpus's CTA, tracking and response path end at Krish's manual action.
7. Offers, pricing, guarantees, bonuses and naming are Krish's and the canon's. The Value Equation, USP test and money model produce strategy proposals only.
8. No fear framing for expensed buyers. Motives are ambition, competence and autonomy.
9. No paid acquisition, generic sequences or automatic connection requests; the Core Four's paid and cold one-to-many-at-scale motions are out.
10. No em dashes in new material, and British English throughout.
11. Measurement uses recorded outcomes only: qualified conversations with human confirmation and paid proofs with payment evidence. Opens, clicks and rubric scores never count as results.
12. Discovery and closing belong to Krish's conversation. The engine prepares questions and records evidence; it never closes or qualifies on its own.
