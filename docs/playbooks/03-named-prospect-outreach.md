# Individual outreach

Write a useful note to one named person about one live decision. Warm and researched cold work are in scope. Bulk sequences, bought lists, automated connection requests and recurring chase schedules are outside this engine.

## Required brief

Before writing, retrieve the exact recipient and sender persona, relationship state, approved contact route, country and recipient type review, suppression state, current buying moment, evidence references, usable proof, decision aid, intended next step and prior relevant messages. Missing contact eligibility blocks the action. Missing evidence blocks the affected sentence.

A named cold draft needs a concrete primary-source observation and a reason this person's role could own the decision. A job title alone does not prove authority. Mark it as an inference until confirmed. Do not write to several people in one business as a substitute for identifying the right owner.

## Choose the argument

Apply [the copy and offer doctrine](10-copy-and-offer-doctrine.md) first: label the path and awareness level, choose the lead type, and hold one decision, one canon promise and one ask.

Start with what changed in their world. Explain one possible consequence with calibrated language. Offer one useful way to think about it. Use proof only if it supports that exact decision and its permission covers the proposed use.

Warm context can support a longer note when each paragraph earns its place. For a cold first contact, keep one observation, one relevance signal, at most one proof point and one low-friction ask. Use at most one link. Rich work means useful reasoning, not a longer product description.

Do not claim an outside-in read describes the business from inside. Its framing must say it is an illustrative outside view, not advice, and something to test against the real business. A decision aid can stand alone even if the recipient never buys.

## Select one next step

- Permission to share a specific aid when the relationship and current ruling support that small ask.
- The free AI brief for a decision owner with a live problem.
- The free publication for someone who is curious and not ready to start.
- A concrete reply or consultation next step already earned in an ongoing conversation.

An introduction permission request belongs to the introduction playbook. A private CTRL invitation belongs to Krish alone. Do not combine routes or add a public diary link. Keep consultation timing in a personal exchange.

## Prepare for manual review

Hand Krish the recipient, channel, subject if relevant, complete body, exact links/attachments, evidence notes, unresolved claims, suppression check and intended action. Keep evidence notes outside the recipient-facing body. The runtime may prepare artifacts; it never sends them.

After Krish manually sends, record the observed action in Control Center with the exact artifact reference and timestamp. Do not mark a draft as sent when it enters a queue. Recheck eligibility and wording if anything changes between review and sending.

## Follow-up rule

A follow-up needs a new useful fact, requested deliverable, actual reply or agreed next step. It reflects the person's words without leaking private material. Each follow-up gets its own draft and exact-action review. No "checking in", manufactured deadline, guilt, repeated CTA or silent channel switch.

Do not recreate the brief service's results and fourteen-day follow-up. A requested human reply is a separate individually reviewed action. Silence is not a trigger for automatic further contact.

## Reject the draft when

It could move unchanged to another recipient; familiarity or urgency lacks evidence; proof mixes attendance with clients; a first-person claim lacks an authorised artifact; it contains a private fee or capacity figure; its claim or route is stale; it asks more than one thing; or the relationship, suppression or lawful-basis review remains unresolved.
