# Relationships and introductions

Help a person notice one leader or decision for which Mindmake may be useful. A connector should not have to explain the whole practice, distribute a campaign or put their reputation behind an unsupported diagnosis.

## Prepare a warm thought

Read the authorised Control Center contact, relationship history, suppression state and relevant company intelligence. Do not infer familiarity from a shared employer, social follow, imported address or attendance record.

Record a private note with the real last touch, the decision that may matter now, source references, what remains uncertain, the value we can provide, and whether contact serves the relationship. Separate "this company changed its pricing" from "this leader needs help". The latter needs a conversation or a clearly labelled hypothesis.

Choose one route: a useful direct note, permission to send a decision aid, a specific introduction request, or no action. A broad request to circulate Mindmake fails the usefulness test.

## Protect the connector

1. Ask the connector whether one relevant introduction would be appropriate. Give a short forwardable explanation of who the work helps, what decision aid could be useful and what the recipient would gain. Include no private brief or diagnosis.
2. The connector separately asks the proposed recipient. Record both opt-ins and their scope privately. One person's yes does not bind the other; silence provides no consent.
3. Only after both opt in does Krish send or accept the exact approved introduction. Share only the context both parties have permitted. If the connector prefers to make it themselves, leave that action with them.
4. Acknowledge the introduction with a useful next step. Keep the connector out of later commercial detail unless both sides ask to include them.

The first request asks for permission. It does not also ask the connector to pitch, forward an attachment and book a meeting. Each proposed action gets a fresh exact draft for Krish to inspect.

## Follow the relationship

Record replies, each opt-in, the agreed next step and any timing promise in Control Center. A future review date prompts the operator to reconsider; it does not schedule a send. When the timing changes, capture that fact. Do not manufacture a follow-up because a fixed number of days passed.

Stop on a no, objection or suppression. Close a silent introduction attempt without guilt or escalating pressure. A different address, new channel or mutual contact never circumvents a person's preference.

## Acceptance

The note identifies a real relationship, a specific decision, a useful contribution and one ask. Its evidence and privacy scope support every included detail. Introduction records show two separate opt-ins before connecting. Krish manually sends the final approved wording; an approved draft alone changes no contact state.
