# Inbound, qualification and paid proofs

Protect the reason someone contacted Mindmake. A free brief request permits the bounded service response; it does not enrol the person in a growth campaign or a publication.

## Handle the request

Read the private fit digest or actual reply, delivery status and requested next step. Use the existing Mindmake service as the source for what the visitor received. Do not send a duplicate acknowledgement, results email or fourteen-day follow-up. If the journey failed and the person asked for help, prepare a human reply that addresses the failure and uses information already supplied.

Summarise the leader's words faithfully. Ask only for missing context that changes the next decision. An unclear problem can be a good starting point; the leader should not have to write a consultant's brief to receive value.

## Qualification standard

A qualified conversation is a held, substantive two-way exchange with a real person, supported by a dated record and human confirmation. All five conditions need evidence:

1. The person owns, or can move, the relevant decision and business result.
2. The person owns a consequential problem, decision or capability to improve.
3. The need is active. General interest in AI is insufficient.
4. Mindmake's offer fits: the work can strengthen judgement or improve a material commercial choice, with access to the context and people needed to test it.
5. The person agrees a concrete next step towards evaluating a bounded paid proof, with an owner and timing recorded privately.

Record `yes`, `no` or `unknown` against each condition with supporting notes and the human confirmer. Unknown never counts as yes. A booked call, brief completed, prepared draft, newsletter subscription, friendly reply or assumed budget does not qualify by itself. Scorecard counting and deduplication are defined in [the scorecard playbook](07-scorecard-and-experiments.md).

Route the outcome: qualify and agree the next decision; request one missing fact; give a useful answer and leave the door open; refer with permission; or close with a reason. Training-only, certification-only, task automation, generic retained availability and production IT requests need a different route rather than a stretched Mindmake promise.

## Consult and scope

State the decision as it now appears and check it with the leader. Identify what the current workaround prevents, what better would look like, and what evidence would disprove the proposed approach. Ask who can move the decision and who must supply context. Do not infer buying authority from title or introduce private buyer psychology into copy.

A proof scope needs:

- one decision or capability and the result it should improve;
- a working first version used on real work;
- explicit exclusions that keep the proof bounded;
- owner, required input, data access and client participation;
- observable acceptance criteria and who judges them;
- the system, reasoning and control the client keeps;
- privately agreed fee, payment terms, dates and scope;
- a decision date and next action, both agreed rather than invented.

Read current private commercial terms from their owner at proposal time. The public repository stores no fee, capacity or client proposal. A proposal draft waits for Krish's exact send approval. Payment and signed scope establish transaction state; optimistic notes do not.

## Close and hand off

Count a paid proof only after a real commercial record identifies this proof and a payment or paid deposit is confirmed in the authorised system. Keep amounts, payer details and transaction evidence private. A verbal yes, sent invoice, signed proposal or scheduled kickoff is insufficient. Flag refunds or payment reversals in the scorecard without erasing history.

Hand the accepted scope, decision evidence, permissions and acceptance criteria to delivery in `mindmake` through the private operating system. Do not migrate client delivery into this growth repository. Close lost or deferred deals with the buyer's actual reason and any agreed revisit condition. Request proof rights separately from commercial acceptance; a paid engagement does not grant publication consent.
