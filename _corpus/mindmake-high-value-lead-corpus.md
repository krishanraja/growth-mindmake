# The Mindmake High-Value Lead System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 16 October 2026  
**Owner:** Krish Raja  
**Purpose:** Build a small, compounding pipeline of high-value Mindmake clients through Krish's network, thoughtful introductions, individually researched outreach, LinkedIn, and useful private assets — without advertising or broad social posting.

> This is an operating corpus, not a campaign plan. It is designed to become the doctrine, data model, research standard, messaging library, cadence, measurement system and quality bar for founder-led business development.

---

## Executive doctrine

Mindmake should not run a conventional lead-generation machine. It should run a **relationship-and-relevance engine**: identify consequential decisions inside businesses Krish can credibly reach; form an evidence-backed point of view about the leader's situation; give that thinking away in a compact, useful form; and ask for either a private conversation or a permission-based introduction.

The central operating idea is:

> **Do not ask the network to distribute Mindmake. Help each person notice one leader or decision for which Mindmake is unusually relevant.**

The method has six moves:

1. **Map the network as paths, not names.** Every record should answer: who is this person, what do they trust Krish for, whom can they reach, and what would make contact useful now?
2. **Prioritise buying moments, not titles.** Founder, CEO, CRO/revenue owner and strategy leader are useful gates, but the real lead is a consequential AI or commercial decision with an accountable owner.
3. **Write a warm thought before a sales message.** The thought is a compact hypothesis about what may have changed, what decision follows, and what Mindmake could help prove.
4. **Give the contact something safe to forward.** A personalised one-pager should be legible to the recipient, an introducer and a hidden internal buyer without extra explanation.
5. **Use a double opt-in introduction.** The connector first checks whether the target wants the introduction. Then Krish replies quickly, moves the connector to BCC and closes the loop later. Specific, forwardable requests reduce work and reputational risk for the connector.[cite:22][cite:75]
6. **Measure decisions advanced, not messages sent.** The system optimises for qualified conversations, paid proofs, useful referrals and relationship strength — not impressions, open rates or list size.

This strategy fits the economics of professional services: trusted referrals often create initial consideration, but reputation, visible expertise and proof still determine whether a referred provider survives diligence.[cite:25][cite:35] It also matches Mindmake's principal-led, tightly bounded delivery model: a few excellent opportunities are worth more than a large pool of weak leads.

## Canonical business truth

### What Mindmake is

Mindmake is a small, principal-led AI and commercial strategy practice run by Krish Raja. It builds a leader's **AI brain** — a working system holding their taste, standards, judgement, memory and trusted context — and can rebuild an **AI-native revenue engine** across product, price, positioning and people. Publicly, the two doors are:

- **Build your AI brain:** The leader's judgement, running as a system they own.
- **Build your AI revenue engine:** Pricing, positioning and team rebuilt for AI, then tested with real buyers.

The public shorthand is:

> **Promote your AI from intern to chief of staff.**

The core category distinction is equally important:

- Not AI training.
- Not a chatbot.
- Not an automation shop.
- Not outsourced thinking.
- Not an open-ended consultancy.
- Not a month of discovery followed by a deck.

The work begins with one important decision or capability, builds a working first version, uses it on real work and leaves the leader with a system they can keep improving. Mindmake's site publicly describes this as a 30-day first proof; price and scope are agreed privately.

### The right buyer

The direct buyer is normally one of:

- Founder or principal.
- CEO or general manager.
- CRO or executive who owns revenue.
- Strategy leader who can move the commercial decision.
- Investor, operating partner or portfolio leader when they can sponsor the decision and reach the accountable operator.

The gate is not seniority alone. A strong prospect must have:

- A decision big enough to matter.
- Authority to move it or direct access to the person who can.
- A business result behind it.
- A reason for action now.
- Enough curiosity and standards to work iteratively.
- A desire to retain understanding, not merely outsource activity.

The strongest current wedge is media, data, creative, publishing and content businesses, because Mindmake's proof and Krish's relationships are strongest there. Software, internet, coaching and advisory businesses can also fit where the decision is material.

### The internal archetype

The most recognisable buyer psychology is the **Accountable Delegator**: a senior commercial leader who has become accountable for AI, can discuss it fluently, but privately knows they are deciding on something they cannot yet do. Their reflex is to delegate AI as though it were IT; the sales task is to reframe AI as a leadership and operating capability without threatening their identity.

Useful signs include:

- Several pilots but no agreed success measures.
- A board, investor or CEO asking for an AI position.
- A vendor decision the leader cannot independently test.
- A technically driven programme with no crisp customer or P&L outcome.
- A pricing, product or positioning model weakened by AI-enabled substitutes.
- A senior leader who is publicly confident and privately uncertain.
- A team waiting on approval because decision rights are unclear.
- Pressure to “do AI” without clarity on the valuable decision.

Never use “Accountable Delegator,” impostor language or psychological diagnosis in external copy. Externally, lead with leadership design, commercial stakes, private experimentation and stronger judgement.

### The buying moments

A lead becomes valuable when one of these moments is visible:

| Buying moment | Observable signal | Mindmake hypothesis | Best door |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build-versus-buy decision | Vendor review, platform partnership, product roadmap conflict | The leader needs a defensible choice and a reasoning trail | AI GTM |
| AI product definition | New AI launch, vague proposition, pilots without adoption | The customer outcome and offer need to become testable | AI GTM |
| Pricing disruption | AI compresses delivery cost or lets customers self-serve | The value unit and price architecture no longer match | AI GTM |
| Positioning drift | Established language no longer explains why the firm matters | Reposition around retained judgement, result or differentiated capability | AI GTM |
| Org redesign | AI hires, restructures, central AI team, approval bottlenecks | Human-AI roles and decision rights need redesign | AI GTM |
| Leader leverage | Founder is bottlenecked; judgement sits in messages, meetings and memory | Capture standards and build a private working system | AI Brain |
| Board pressure | Board request, investor thesis, public commitment or budget review | Turn AI narrative into one evidenced, ownable decision | Either |
| Tool sprawl | Multiple pilots, licences or agents without a coherent system | Stop weak bets and prove one useful capability | Either |
| Market movement | Competitor launch, regulation, search/platform change, funding shift | Decide before the external signal becomes consensus | AI GTM |
| Succession or scale | Founder cannot remain in every decision | Encode enough judgement to increase leverage without erasing agency | AI Brain |

### Disqualifiers

Deprioritise or decline when:

- The person cannot move the relevant decision.
- The ask is generic AI training, certification or prompt tips.
- The buyer only wants a list of automations.
- The requirement is production IT implementation rather than commercial decision-making.
- There is no named business result.
- The desired relationship is an unbounded fractional role.
- The prospect wants Krish to “do AI for us” while remaining disengaged.
- The expected value cannot justify principal-led attention.
- Procurement must happen before a sponsor is identified.
- Contact would rely on private, sensitive or surprising data.

## Strategy architecture

### The three lanes

Run three distinct acquisition lanes. Do not blur their tone or operating rules.

| Lane | Who belongs | Primary ask | Personalisation | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm direct** | People who know and trust Krish | A brief exchange about a live issue, or permission to send a one-pager | Relationship memory plus current signal | Human, sparse, conversational |
| **Warm path** | Target is reachable through a trusted connector | Permission for a double opt-in introduction | Target brief plus why connector is credible | Connector first; no target contact until permission |
| **Cold precise** | No credible path, but unusually strong fit and trigger | Permission to send a short point of view or compare notes | Deep account, role and trigger research | Maximum 3–4 useful touches; stop cleanly |

The order of effort is warm direct, then warm path, then cold precise. LinkedIn is used for research, context, public proof and individual contact — not volume posting.

### The path model

A network is not one list. It is a graph with four useful node types:

- **Buyer:** Can commission the work.
- **Champion:** Feels the problem and can mobilise the buyer.
- **Connector:** Can credibly introduce Krish to the buyer or champion.
- **Amplifier:** Can validate the idea privately or publicly, host a conversation, or circulate an asset.

One person may occupy more than one role. Record each plausible path explicitly:

`Krish → connector → champion → economic buyer → hidden buyers`

For every target account, identify:

- Shortest trusted path.
- Strongest trust path.
- Most context-rich path.
- Lowest-reputational-risk path.
- Alternate path if the first route is unavailable.

Never ask a weak tie to make a strong endorsement. Match the ask to the strength of the relationship:

| Relationship | Safe ask |
|---|---|
| Strong, recent trust | “Would you be comfortable checking whether X is open to an introduction?” |
| Strong but dormant | Reconnect around them first; later ask for perspective or a narrow intro |
| Collegial acquaintance | Ask whether the hypothesis sounds relevant, not for an endorsement |
| Public-only connection | Treat as cold precise; do not imply a relationship |
| Former client with demonstrated result | Ask after confirming the value moment; make the desired profile specific |

### The account unit

Treat the account — not the individual email address — as the operational unit. A high-value decision normally includes visible buyers and hidden influencers. Edelman and LinkedIn's 2025 research found that hidden buyers consume and use thought leadership, often have little sales contact and can advocate internally when the material helps them reframe a problem.[cite:62][cite:64]

Each account record should include:

- Account thesis.
- Named decision or likely decision.
- Economic buyer.
- Champion.
- Hidden buyers: finance, operations, technology, legal, procurement, product or board.
- Warm paths.
- Trigger evidence.
- Relevant proof.
- Potential gift of insight.
- Next smallest useful action.

## Prioritisation system

### Fit score

Score 0–5 on each dimension. Weight business stakes and access more heavily than superficial engagement.

| Dimension | Weight | Scoring question |
|---|---:|---|
| Decision authority | 20 | Can this person commission or materially move the work? |
| Consequence | 20 | Would a better decision materially change revenue, cost, risk or strategic position? |
| Trigger strength | 15 | Is there a current, evidenced reason to act? |
| Mindmake fit | 15 | Is this a judgement, capability or GTM problem rather than commodity execution? |
| Trust/access | 15 | Is there direct trust or a credible warm path? |
| Proof adjacency | 10 | Does Mindmake have a relevant, permitted result or experience? |
| Curiosity/standards | 5 | Does the leader appear willing to experiment and retain agency? |

Calculate:

`Opportunity score = Σ (dimension score / 5 × weight)`

Then apply these gates:

- **Tier A, 80–100:** Research now; founder writes; pursue one deliberate path.
- **Tier B, 65–79:** Develop signal and relationship; ask connector for perspective.
- **Tier C, 50–64:** Nurture privately; monitor for trigger.
- **Below 50:** Park. Do not manufacture urgency.

### Confidence score

Keep fit and confidence separate. A compelling hypothesis based on weak evidence is not a strong lead.

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Direct statement by buyer, filing, earnings call, job brief or verified conversation |
| 4 | Multiple current public signals agree |
| 3 | One credible current signal plus strong contextual fit |
| 2 | Role- or sector-level inference only |
| 1 | Speculation |

Only personalise assertively at confidence 4–5. At confidence 2–3, use tentative language: “I may be reading this wrong,” “it looks as if,” and “one possibility.” Never present inference as fact.

### Relationship budget

Every outreach consumes trust. Assign a simple relationship budget:

- **Green:** Recent reciprocity, positive interaction or explicit invitation.
- **Amber:** Dormant but genuine relationship; reconnect before asking.
- **Red:** Weak context, unresolved history, recent layoff/crisis, or repeated non-response.

A high fit score does not override a red relationship state.

## Signal intelligence

### What to monitor

Use only relevant, proportionate and ethically obtained information:

- Company announcements and strategy pages.
- Earnings calls, investor materials and public interviews.
- Leadership appointments and mandate changes.
- Hiring patterns for AI, data, product, strategy or transformation.
- Product launches, pricing changes and partnerships.
- Acquisition, fundraising, restructuring or geographic expansion.
- Executive LinkedIn posts and comments.
- Conference appearances and podcast interviews.
- Changes in the company's customers, platforms or regulatory environment.
- Krish's own relationship history, where appropriate and accurately recorded.

Do not scrape or store personal-life details, sensitive data, inferred protected characteristics, or information that would feel invasive if repeated back.

### Trigger quality

A trigger is useful only if it changes the probability, stakes or timing of a decision.

**Strong triggers:**

- “We are reviewing build versus partner.”
- CEO publicly commits to an AI-enabled product.
- CRO hired with a transformation mandate.
- Company launches several AI pilots.
- Major platform change undermines the current revenue model.
- Team advertises roles that reveal a new operating model.
- Board or investor asks for an AI plan.

**Weak triggers:**

- “They posted about AI.”
- “They use ChatGPT.”
- “Their industry is being disrupted.”
- Generic funding news with no connection to Mindmake's work.
- Job title alone.

### The three-signal rule

For Tier A cold or warm-path accounts, seek three independent forms of relevance:

1. **Person signal:** Mandate, stated belief, career move or problem ownership.
2. **Company signal:** Strategic move, launch, hiring, economics or operational change.
3. **Market signal:** External change that raises the cost of delay or creates a new option.

The outreach need not mention all three. The research brief should.

## The warm thought

### Definition

A **warm thought** is an unsolicited but genuinely useful piece of judgement prepared for one person. It is not flattery, a disguised pitch or a generic AI idea. It should make the recipient think, “That is an intelligent reading of the decision in front of us.”

A strong warm thought contains:

- **Observation:** What changed, using one or two verifiable signals.
- **Interpretation:** Why that change may matter commercially.
- **Decision:** The concrete choice it creates for the leader.
- **Possibility:** One useful route worth testing.
- **Boundary:** What is not yet known.
- **Offer:** A low-friction next step that leaves control with them.

Template:

> I noticed **[specific change]**. My read — and I may be wrong — is that this creates a **[decision]** problem more than a **[obvious but weaker framing]** problem. The interesting move may be **[possibility]**, because **[commercial reason]**. I sketched the logic on one page. Happy to send it; no need for a call unless it is useful.

### Quality test

Do not send unless the thought passes all seven tests:

1. **Specific:** Could this be sent unchanged to another leader? If yes, rewrite.
2. **Material:** Does it touch revenue, strategic position, decision quality or leadership leverage?
3. **Evidence-based:** Can each observation be traced to a source?
4. **Tentative:** Are assumptions clearly distinguished from facts?
5. **Useful alone:** Would the thought still help if the person never buys?
6. **Mindmake-shaped:** Does it point toward retained judgement and a working proof, not generic implementation?
7. **Human:** Does it sound like Krish noticed and thought, rather than a model assembled fields?

### Five levels

|---|---|---|
| 0. Merge | Name, company | Never sufficient |
| 1. Role | Mandate and likely responsibilities | Tier C only |
| 2. Account | Company strategy, product or market movement | Minimum for cold outreach |

### AI's role

AI may:

- Gather and reconcile public signals.
- Draft a structured research brief.
- Suggest hypotheses and disconfirming evidence.
- Match permitted proof to the situation.
- Produce message variants.
- Check claims, tone, privacy and duplication.

## The one-pager system

### Purpose

The one-pager is not a brochure. It is a **portable decision aid**. Its job is to be useful to the initial contact and easy for them to forward to a potential buyer or hidden influencer.

Professional-services referrals still undergo digital diligence; buyers can reject referred firms before a conversation if the website, ideas or proof do not support the introduction.[cite:25][cite:33] The one-pager therefore needs to bridge personal trust and institutional credibility.

### Master structure

Keep it to one page, ideally 450–700 words.

1. **Title:** Name the decision, not Mindmake.
2. **Why now:** Two or three current signals.
3. **The hidden decision:** The choice behind the visible activity.
4. **Three questions:** Questions the leadership team should be able to answer.
5. **A 30-day proof:** What could be built and tested on real work.
6. **What stays:** The result, reasoning and working system the leader keeps.
7. **Relevant proof:** One permitted case, accurately framed.
8. **About Mindmake:** Two sentences.
9. **Invitation:** “If this is live, Krish is happy to compare notes privately.”

### Title formulas

- **The AI decision underneath [company's visible move]**
- **What [market shift] changes about [company's product/pricing/team]**
- **A 30-day way to test [decision] before committing to [large programme]**
- **From [current state] to one decision the team can run**
- **What the leader should keep when AI carries more of the work**

### Proof matching

Use only a case that resembles the decision, not merely the sector.

| Prospect situation | Best public-safe proof |
|---|---|
| Build versus partner | Two quarters of debate resolved in a day; partnership signed the following month |
| Unclear offer | Advisory expertise turned into a buyable offer and launch plan |
| Product/pricing confusion | Positioning and pricing rebuilt; two pilots signed during the work |
| Founder bottleneck | Business systems rebuilt and handed back to the founder |
| Founder-led thought leadership | Publishing time reduced from days to under an hour; cadence moved from monthly to most days |
| Vendor sprawl | Fourteen vendors reduced to three decisions; the client's team shipped with no new hires |
| Tool sprawl and weak business link | Eleven of fourteen tools stopped; one system went live inside 90 days |
| Market/business-model change | New AI-native sales path led to a paid test with a major US publisher |

All proof needs to preserve its approved attribution level and exact consent. Session attendance is not client proof; named career references are not AI outcomes; anonymous client stories must stay anonymous.

### The forwardability test

Before attaching, ask:

- Can the connector understand whom this is for in ten seconds?
- Can they forward it without writing a sales pitch on Krish's behalf?
- Would the recipient learn something before encountering the CTA?
- Is there one decision, not a list of Mindmake capabilities?
- Does the document make clear what is observed, inferred and unknown?
- Is there one relevant proof point?
- Is there no public price, unapproved client name or private identifier?
- Is the filename neutral and useful, e.g. `company-ai-decision-brief.pdf`?

## Warm-network plays

### Play 1: Direct relevance

Use when Krish knows the buyer and has a credible thought about a current decision.

**Message**

> **Subject:** A thought on [decision]
>
> [Name] — saw [specific change]. It made me wonder whether the real decision is [decision], rather than [surface activity].
>
> I sketched a one-page view of what I would test before committing further. Happy to send it if useful. No pitch attached to the offer.

If they say yes, send the asset and ask one question. Do not immediately convert permission to send into a meeting request.

### Play 2: Reconnect before relevance

Use when the relationship is real but dormant.

**Message**

> **Subject:** Been too long
>
> [Name] — I was thinking about [specific shared work/person/moment] after seeing [current relevant event]. How are things on your side?
>
> I have been building Mindmake around a problem that keeps showing up for leaders: AI can carry more work, but their own judgement is rarely captured in the system. Your world came to mind for a specific reason, but I will save the work talk until I have heard how you are.

This is not a trick. If the only reason to reconnect is the ask, do not pretend otherwise; use the direct transparent version.

### Play 3: Ask for perspective

Use when the contact understands the market but is not the buyer.

> I have a hypothesis that [specific leader type] is getting stuck at [decision], especially when [trigger]. You see more of this market than I do. Does that ring true, or am I over-reading it?

If they validate it, ask whom the situation brings to mind. Perspective first can create a more natural referral than an immediate generic request.

### Play 4: Named warm path

Use when the target and connector are known.

> [Connector] — I noticed [target/company signal], and I think [target] may be facing a specific [decision] call. You know both of us, so I wanted your read before doing anything: does that sound live and relevant?
>
> If yes, would you be comfortable checking whether they would welcome an introduction? I have put a forwardable note below so this takes one minute. Absolutely fine if not.

**Forwardable note**

> [Target] — I know Krish Raja through [basis]. He runs Mindmake, a principal-led practice that helps leaders turn consequential AI and commercial decisions into a working proof they keep.
>
> He noticed [specific signal] and has a short thought about [decision]. It sounded relevant to what you are doing. Would you be open to an introduction? No problem if the timing is wrong.

Specific targets, a precise reason and a forwardable email reduce friction for the introducer.[cite:22][cite:75]

### Play 5: Open referral prompt

Use with someone who knows Krish's work but no target has been selected.

Avoid: “Do you know anyone who might be interested?”

Use:

> I am looking for two leaders who own a consequential AI or commercial decision — usually a founder, CEO, CRO or strategy lead — and who want to prove one move on real work rather than buy training or another deck. Who comes to mind as already dealing with that?

Then narrow with one trigger:

> Especially anyone deciding build versus buy, resetting an AI-era offer or pricing model, or trying to capture a founder's judgement before the team scales.

The ask gives the contact a memory cue. Referral guidance consistently recommends specifying the target person, problem and introduction process rather than asking for “anyone.”[cite:78][cite:81][cite:85]

### Play 6: Client value moment

Ask only after a genuine result, praise, handoff or renewal — not during onboarding or unresolved delivery.

> Thank you — the fact that [specific result] is now working matters a lot. I am trying to find one or two other leaders facing the same kind of [decision]. Who do you know who is currently dealing with [specific situation]?
>
> If someone comes to mind, I can write a two-line opt-in note for you. And if there is anyone I can usefully connect for you, tell me.

Asking just after demonstrated value is a recurring best practice in B2B referral systems.[cite:78][cite:81]

### Play 7: Connector council

Build a small group of 10–20 trusted connectors across media, data, software, creative industries, investors, executive search, legal, finance and specialist advisory. The purpose is not a formal affiliate scheme. It is reciprocal market intelligence.

Quarterly, send each connector:

- One useful market observation tailored to their world.
- One precise profile Mindmake can help.
- One offer to make an introduction or help them think through a situation.
- One update on a past introduction, without disclosing confidential details.

Reciprocity and closing the loop preserve the relationship better than transactional bounty language.[cite:87][cite:90]

### Play 8: Past-colleague map

For each former company or ecosystem:

- Identify 5–10 people who trusted Krish's commercial or technical judgement.
- Record current roles and companies.
- Map who now owns product, revenue, AI, strategy or transformation.
- Look for a current trigger before contacting.
- Reconnect with a thought tied to their new mandate, not nostalgia alone.

The history is context, not entitlement. Never imply that a former colleague owes an introduction.

### Play 9: Private roundtable

Use selectively for 5–8 senior leaders who share one live decision. No public event promotion is required.

Format:

- One hard question, e.g. “What should a leader still understand when agents do the work?”
- 60 minutes, no slides.
- Chatham House Rule if appropriate.
- Participants contribute one live tension.
- Krish circulates a two-page synthesis afterward.
- Follow-up is individual and only where a relevant issue emerged.

This creates value and reveals demand without turning the room into a sales webinar.

### Play 10: Give an introduction first

Before asking a strategic connector for access, look for one useful introduction Krish can make for them. Do it because it is genuinely useful, not as a token. Record reciprocal value so the system does not become extractive.

## Cold precise outreach

### When cold is justified

Use cold outreach only when:

- Fit is Tier A or strong Tier B.
- A current trigger is verified.
- The hypothesis is decision-specific.
- The recipient is a corporate business contact or another lawful contact route is available.
- There is no credible warm path, or the connector declines.
- Krish can offer a useful thought without needing a meeting.

Generic volume is strategically wrong for Mindmake. Small, targeted campaigns and signal-based personalisation routinely outperform broad, generic sends in current outbound benchmarks, although vendor-reported figures vary and should be treated directionally.[cite:23][cite:29]

### The four-touch ceiling

Use a restrained sequence. Each touch must add information.

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Email or LinkedIn DM | Specific observation and permission to send the thought |
| 4–5 | Other channel if context exists | Add one useful question or signal; no “bumping this” |
| 10–12 | Email | Send a compact insight or relevant case; clarify why it may matter |
| 21 | Email | Close the loop and release them |

Stop earlier on any “no,” objection, opt-out, mismatch or indication of sensitive timing. Never continue because a sequence says so.

### Cold opener

> **Subject:** [Company] and the [decision]
>
> [Name] — [specific observable signal] caught my attention.
>
> My read may be wrong, but it looks less like a [surface problem] and more like a decision about [material decision]. I have a one-page view of what I would test before [commitment/risk].
>
> Useful if I send it?
>
> Krish

### Follow-up with value

> One addition, then I will leave this with you: [new evidence or concise implication].
>
> It is why I think the first proof should test [one thing], not start with [large programme]. Happy to send the page if relevant; otherwise no need to reply.

### Close-the-loop

> I may have misread the timing, so I will close this here. If [decision/trigger] becomes live later, I am happy to share the brief. Either way, wishing you well with [specific initiative].

### LinkedIn rules

LinkedIn can support:

- Research and shared-connection discovery.
- A genuine connection request with clear context.
- Direct messages to warm contacts or relevant corporate buyers.
- Occasional high-quality posts that establish the point of view behind the outreach.
- Visible proof through recommendations, case studies and profile clarity.

LinkedIn itself recommends shared connections, contextual research and value-led interactions for warm introductions.[cite:38] Do not automate connection requests, fake engagement or use comments as covert pitches.

## LinkedIn without content treadmill

The constraint is no broad social posting except LinkedIn. Treat LinkedIn as a **trust surface**, not the lead engine itself.

### Minimum credible profile

The profile should answer in 15 seconds:

- Whom Krish helps.
- Which high-stakes AI/commercial decisions he helps with.
- What the buyer keeps.
- Why his judgement is credible.
- The one next step.

Recommended structure:

- **Headline:** Principal-led AI and commercial strategy for leaders making consequential decisions | Build your AI brain or AI-native revenue engine.
- **About opening:** “Every AI knows the market. None knows the leader. Mindmake builds the one that does.”
- **Featured:** Homepage, strongest matched case study, one canonical one-pager, free AI brief.
- **Experience:** Mindmake as the current practice, with two doors and clear exclusions.
- **Recommendations:** Seek current, consented recommendations that speak to judgement, translation, useful outcomes and hand-back.

### Posting rhythm

Maximum useful rhythm:

- One substantial point of view every 1–2 weeks.
- One short field note when a genuine pattern appears.
- One case-led post per month, using permitted proof.
- Thoughtful comments on a small number of relevant leaders' posts, only when there is something to add.

The objective is that a referred buyer who checks Krish sees coherent thinking. Thought leadership can increase receptivity among visible and hidden buyers, especially when it reveals an unrecognised risk or challenges an assumption.[cite:56][cite:66]

### Post-to-outreach link

A post should never become an excuse to blast everyone who liked it. Instead:

1. Publish only if the idea stands alone.
2. Note relevant inbound comments or messages.
3. Reply in public where useful.
4. Move private only with context or permission.
5. Add the insight to future one-pagers and research briefs.

## Message library

### Permission to send a one-pager

> I have a short view on [decision] based on [signal]. It may be useful and it may be wrong. Shall I send the one-page version?

### Send after permission

> Here it is. The important bit is page one — specifically the distinction between [A] and [B]. What am I missing from inside the business?

### Ask for a named introduction

> You know [Name] better than I do. I have a specific hypothesis about [decision] at [Company], based on [signal]. Would you be comfortable checking whether they are open to an introduction? I can write the opt-in note, and “not relevant” is completely fine.

### Ask who comes to mind

> Who do you know who currently owns an AI decision that is commercially important but still hard to explain — build versus buy, a product/pricing reset, or how the team should change? That is where Mindmake is most useful.

### Referral via past client

> The result we reached on [approved description] is exactly the kind of proof I want to repeat. Who else do you know facing that same decision? I will handle any introduction carefully and check fit before discussing work.

### Connector declines

> Understood — thank you for being direct. I will not approach through you. If the situation changes, you can always reopen it; no action needed.

### Target is interested

> Thanks, [Connector] — moving you to BCC so you are not trapped in logistics.
>
> [Target], good to meet you. I will send the one-page thought first so you can decide whether a conversation is worth it. If it is, I can do [two specific options].

### Target is curious, not ready

> Makes sense. The useful next step may simply be to sharpen the decision, not start work. If you send me the one sentence version, I will reply with the two or three questions I would use to test it.

### “Send information”

> Absolutely. Rather than send a general deck, which of these is closer: capturing your own judgement as a working AI system, or rebuilding a product/pricing/positioning/team decision for AI? I will send the relevant one-pager and one case.

### “We already have consultants/vendors”

> Good — this is not a replacement claim. The question is whether the leader's own understanding and decision system are compounding while the work moves. Mindmake can prove one missing capability alongside the existing team, then leave it in your hands.

### “We have ChatGPT/Claude/Copilot”

> That solves access to a capable model. The Mindmake question is whether it knows your standards, past decisions, trusted context and method well enough to improve real work — and whether that capability belongs to you rather than one tool.

### “We need training”

> Training may help the team, but Mindmake starts elsewhere: one consequential decision or capability, built and tested on real work. If the need is primarily curriculum or certification, it is probably not the right fit.

### “No budget”

> Understood. Then it is not useful to force a sales process. Keep the brief; if the decision becomes material enough to fund a proof, we can revisit it.

### Break-up note

> I am closing this loop so the message does not become noise. The thought stands if [trigger] becomes live later. No reply needed.

## Conversation design

### The first call

A first conversation is not a discovery interrogation. Its job is to decide whether there is one valuable proof.

Use this arc:

1. **Why this, why now?** What changed?
2. **Name the decision.** What choice is the leader actually accountable for?
3. **Quantify the consequence.** What happens if it is wrong or delayed?
4. **Locate understanding.** What does the leader know that the tools, vendors or team do not?
5. **Test authority.** Who can move the decision, and who can stop it?
6. **Choose one brick.** What useful first version could be working in 30 days?
7. **Define proof.** What would the client use, endorse or keep?
8. **Agree next action.** Proceed, involve another stakeholder, send a scoped note, or close.

### Questions worth asking

- What decision keeps getting discussed without becoming clearer?
- What has AI changed about the customer's alternative?
- Which part of this can only you currently judge?
- What are vendors asking you to believe that you cannot yet test?
- If the next 30 days produced one useful thing, what would the team actually use?
- Who must believe the result for it to move?
- What should your organisation still understand when an external partner leaves?
- What would make this obviously not worth continuing?

### Qualification outcome

Every conversation ends in one state:

- **Paid-proof candidate:** Decision, owner, stakes and first brick are clear.
- **Stakeholder step:** Fit exists, but another decision-maker must join.
- **Nurture:** Fit exists, trigger or resources do not.
- **Referral:** Not a buyer, but a credible connector or partner.
- **No fit:** Close respectfully and record why.

## Pipeline operating system

### Required objects

**Person**

- Name, role, company, location.
- Relationship strength and history.
- Trust basis.
- Buyer/champion/connector/amplifier roles.
- Communication preference.
- Lawful basis and privacy status.
- Do-not-contact state.

**Account**

- Sector, size, business model and geography.
- Strategic priorities.
- Trigger events and sources.
- Decision hypothesis.
- Buying group.
- Fit, confidence and relationship scores.

**Path**

- From person.
- To person/account.
- Path strength.
- Permission status.
- Requested action.
- Reputational risk.

**Opportunity**

- Named decision.
- Economic consequence.
- Door: Brain or GTM.
- Proposed 30-day proof.
- Relevant proof.
- Stage and next action.
- Expected value and probability.

**Touch**

- Date and channel.
- Message or conversation summary.
- Value delivered.
- Response and sentiment.
- Explicit commitments.
- Next step and owner.

### Stages

1. **Observed:** Relevant person/account exists.
2. **Triggered:** Current evidence suggests a buying moment.
3. **Researched:** Brief and confidence score complete.
4. **Path selected:** Direct, warm path or cold precise.
5. **Permission sought:** Connector or target asked appropriately.
6. **Value delivered:** Warm thought or one-pager received.
7. **Conversation:** Decision discussed.
8. **Qualified:** Owner, stakes, first proof and process clear.
9. **Scoped:** Private scope and fee prepared.
10. **Paid proof:** Agreement reached.
11. **Continuation/referral:** Result creates next deliverable or relationship path.
12. **Closed/parked:** Reason recorded; future trigger defined if relevant.

### The daily queue

Do not show Krish a database. Show a decision queue:

- **Think:** 3 accounts needing a warm thought.
- **Ask:** 3 contacts where permission or an introduction is appropriate.
- **Reply:** All human responses requiring same-day attention.
- **Prepare:** 1–2 one-pagers or call briefs.
- **Close:** Stale loops that should be released.
- **Thank:** Connectors and clients owed an update.

### Weekly rhythm

**Monday: Prioritise**

- Review new triggers.
- Re-score top 25 accounts.
- Select at most five founder-worthy moves.

**Tuesday: Research**

- Complete three Tier A briefs.
- Find disconfirming evidence.
- Match proof and path.

**Wednesday: Create**

- Write warm thoughts.
- Produce no more than two personalised one-pagers.
- Prepare forwardable intro notes.

**Thursday: Converse**

- Contact warm direct leads and connectors.
- Hold qualified calls.
- Reply to every human response.

**Friday: Learn**

- Close loops with connectors.
- Record objections and language.
- Review conversion by trigger, path, buyer and asset.
- Decide what the system should stop doing.

### Capacity constraint

Mindmake's canon caps simultaneous new paid proofs. Therefore cap active founder-led prospecting too:

- 25 Tier A/B accounts in active observation.
- 10 researched opportunities ready for contact.
- 5 deliberate first touches per week.
- 2 bespoke one-pagers per week.
- 3–5 first conversations per week maximum.

These are operating limits, not promises or benchmarks. Increase only when delivery capacity and response quality support it.

## Measurement

### North-star metric

**Qualified decisions advanced per month**

A qualified decision is advanced when Mindmake helps an authorised leader move from vague concern to a named decision, next test or paid proof.

### Funnel metrics

| Layer | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Network | Active trusted connectors; reciprocal value given; paths with permission |
| Relevance | Triggered accounts; researched briefs; confidence distribution |
| Outreach | Permission rate; positive reply rate; opt-out/negative response rate |
| Value | One-pagers accepted; forwarded; discussed by additional stakeholders |
| Conversation | Qualified-decision rate; stakeholder expansion; no-fit rate |
| Commercial | Scopes issued; paid proofs; cycle length; expected and realised value |
| Relationship | Introducer satisfaction; loops closed; repeat introductions; client referrals |
| Learning | Objections; disproved hypotheses; proof gaps; messages retired |

Do not optimise open rate. Privacy features and mail clients make it noisy, and it says little about decision quality. Positive replies, qualified conversations and introductions are more useful.

### Cohort cuts

Review results by:

- Warm direct vs warm path vs cold precise.
- Buyer role.
- Sector.
- Buying moment.
- Trigger type.
- AI Brain vs AI GTM door.
- Proof story used.
- One-pager vs no one-pager.
- Relationship age and strength.
- Message author: fully human vs AI-assisted/human-approved.

### Diagnostic ratios

- **Permission rate:** Permissions granted / permission requests.
- **Warm-path activation:** Introductions made / connector asks.
- **Positive reply rate:** Useful positive replies / delivered first touches.
- **Decision rate:** Conversations with a named consequential decision / first conversations.
- **Proof rate:** Paid proofs / qualified decisions.
- **Referral yield:** Qualified introductions / referral asks.
- **Relationship debt:** Open loops owed to connectors / total active introductions.

Never create target benchmarks without a Mindmake baseline. Use the first 50 deliberate approaches to establish channel-specific evidence.

## Experiments

Run experiments at the level of strategy, not punctuation. Minimum useful sample sizes will often be small, so combine quantitative outcomes with qualitative review.

### Experiment backlog

1. **Ask type:** Permission to send one-pager vs direct meeting request.
2. **Path:** Named-target intro vs open “who comes to mind?” prompt.
3. **Frame:** AI Brain vs AI GTM for principals who could fit either.
4. **Warm thought:** Decision hypothesis vs market observation.
5. **Proof:** Decision-adjacent case vs sector-adjacent case.
6. **Asset:** Company-specific one-pager vs canonical decision guide.
7. **Call CTA:** 20-minute compare-notes call vs written reply to one question.
8. **Connector value:** Insight first vs introduction offered first.
9. **LinkedIn:** Public post preceding outreach vs private-only insight.
10. **Sequence:** Two high-value touches vs four-touch close-out sequence.

### Experiment record

```yaml
hypothesis:
audience:
change:
control:
primary_metric:
quality_guardrail:
start_date:
end_date:
results:
qualitative_notes:
decision: adopt|adapt|stop|insufficient_evidence
```

Do not call a winner based on one senior reply. Record the story, but distinguish anecdote from pattern.

## Compliance and trust

### UK direct-marketing boundary

This corpus is operational guidance, not legal advice. Before launch, document the lawful basis and have the privacy approach reviewed where needed.

Key practical rules:

- B2B direct marketing still involves data-protection obligations when processing named business contacts.[cite:45][cite:47]
- Under PECR, electronic-mail rules differ between corporate subscribers and individual subscribers such as sole traders and some partnerships; corporate B2B email may not require PECR consent, but UK GDPR still requires a lawful basis.[cite:39][cite:45]
- If relying on legitimate interests, conduct and document the purpose, necessity and balancing tests; contact must be targeted, proportionate and within reasonable expectations.[cite:46][cite:48]
- Clearly identify Mindmake/Krish, explain the use of personal data through an accessible privacy notice, provide an easy objection/opt-out route and honour it promptly.[cite:49][cite:71]
- Maintain a suppression list and screen every send against it.[cite:42][cite:49]
- When personal data comes from public or third-party sources, transparency obligations may require disclosing the categories and source within the applicable period.[cite:68][cite:71]
- LinkedIn DMs are electronic messages; do not assume social messaging sits outside direct-marketing rules.[cite:41][cite:42]
- Never encourage uncontrolled forwarding of marketing emails to personal contacts. Use a double opt-in introduction request instead.[cite:42]

### Data minimisation

Store only what helps determine relevance, permission, relationship and follow-up. Apply retention rules:

- Review inactive unqualified contact records after 12 months.
- Keep suppression records as needed to honour objections.
- Delete speculative notes that are sensitive, unfair or no longer necessary.
- Separate factual source notes from Krish's interpretation.
- Restrict private pricing, buyer psychology and engagement records.
- Do not put private identifiers or unapproved client information in shareable assets.

### Sending and deliverability

- All sends require Krish's explicit approval immediately before sending.
- Use the authorised Mindmake/client-safe business identity; do not use a personal address for client work.
- Authenticate the domain. Google requires SPF or DKIM for all senders to Gmail and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders, alongside alignment and low spam rates.[cite:67][cite:70]
- Use plain-text or light HTML for individual executive outreach.
- Validate addresses lawfully and cautiously; remove hard bounces immediately.
- Never conceal sender identity.
- Avoid tracking pixels where they add privacy cost but little decision value.
- Do not send from multiple lookalike domains or manufacture volume.

### Approval gate

Immediately before any action that sends, publishes, spends, deletes or changes permissions, present:

- Recipient(s) and relationship.
- Exact message/asset.
- Channel and timing.
- Source and lawful-basis status.
- Claims/proof check.
- Opt-out/suppression check.
- Expected next action.

Krish then approves that specific action. Prior approval of a strategy is not approval of a send.

## Proof governance

Use Mindmake proof as evidence, not decoration.

### Rules

- Quotes are verbatim; do not silently improve grammar or wording.
- Short excerpts must be exact substrings.
- Keep anonymous clients anonymous unless explicit consent says otherwise.
- Attendance logos prove attendance, not advisory clients.
- Career references prove Krish's way of working, not a Mindmake client outcome.
- Do not reuse internal pricing, private buyer psychology or engagement details externally.
- Do not infer a result beyond the approved wording.
- Match a proof to the decision it demonstrates.
- Record exactly which proof was used in each outreach asset.

### Proof-selection question

> What doubt does this buyer need resolved?

Then choose:

- “Can he make a difficult choice legible?” → build/partner case.
- “Can this become commercially buyable?” → offer or positioning/pricing case.
- “Will he leave capability, not dependence?” → hand-back or owned-system case.
- “Can a large team stop dabbling?” → vendor/tool-sprawl cases.
- “Can he spot and act on market change?” → paid-publisher-test case.

## Automation design

### Safe architecture

1. **Ingest:** Contacts, relationship notes, company records and permitted public signals.
2. **Resolve:** Deduplicate people and companies; maintain source provenance.
3. **Detect:** Flag possible triggers.
4. **Research:** Generate a brief with facts, citations, uncertainty and contrary evidence.
5. **Score:** Fit, confidence, relationship budget and compliance status.
6. **Route:** Warm direct, warm path, cold precise, nurture or no-fit.
7. **Draft:** Warm thought, one-pager and optional forwardable intro.
8. **Verify:** Claims, proof permission, tone, privacy, duplication and CTA.
9. **Approve:** Krish reviews the exact action immediately before send.
10. **Record:** Capture response, commitments and next step.
11. **Learn:** Update message, trigger and proof performance.

### Human checkpoints

Mandatory human judgement at:

- Relationship interpretation.
- Whether the hypothesis is worth saying.
- Any use of private history.
- Proof selection.
- Final message and attachment.
- Every send.
- Qualification and scope.
- Referral thank-you and connector update.

### Failure states

Stop the workflow when:

- Source cannot be verified.
- Person/account match is ambiguous.
- Relationship note conflicts with current data.
- Trigger is older than the allowed freshness window.
- Personalisation relies on sensitive data.
- Consent/legitimate-interest status is unknown where required.
- Suppression status is unknown.
- Proof permission is uncertain.
- The message could be sent to another prospect unchanged.
- The system tries to schedule or send without explicit approval.

## 90-day launch plan

### Days 1–14: Foundation

- Consolidate the network into Person, Account and Path objects.
- Deduplicate identities and preserve provenance.
- Mark relationship strength, trust basis and last meaningful interaction.
- Build the initial buyer/champion/connector/amplifier map.
- Write the privacy notice, legitimate-interests assessment where applicable, retention rule and suppression process.
- Verify email authentication and sending identity.
- Create canonical AI Brain, AI GTM and warm-introduction one-pagers.
- Create a proof-permission lookup using the canonical Mindmake proof file.
- Select 25 accounts and 20 strategic connectors.

**Exit criterion:** Every active record has a source, relationship state, compliance state and next-action owner.

### Days 15–30: Pilot

- Complete ten research briefs.
- Write five warm thoughts.
- Ask three named connectors for perspective or opt-in introductions.
- Contact two warm-direct buyers.
- Produce no more than four personalised one-pagers.
- Hold first conversations and record language verbatim where appropriate.
- Publish at most one LinkedIn point of view supporting the core thesis.

**Exit criterion:** At least ten real responses or explicit non-responses have produced usable learning about triggers, asks and proof.

### Days 31–60: Refine

- Review outcomes by lane and buying moment.
- Retire generic or low-signal messages.
- Deepen the two strongest buyer situations.
- Add a connector closing-the-loop workflow.
- Build objection cards from real conversations.
- Create two vertical decision briefs for the strongest sectors.
- Test permission-to-send against direct-call CTA.
- Convene one small private roundtable only if five relevant leaders genuinely fit the same question.

**Exit criterion:** One repeatable warm-path play and one repeatable cold-precise play produce qualified conversations without damaging relationships.

### Days 61–90: Compound

- Focus active outreach on the top two triggers and best proof pairings.
- Ask for referrals at genuine value moments.
- Establish a quarterly connector council rhythm.
- Add stakeholder maps to qualified accounts.
- Turn common one-pager logic into reusable research modules, not generic templates.
- Establish the first Mindmake-specific baselines for permission, positive reply, qualified decision and paid-proof conversion.
- Decide what to automate next based on demonstrated bottlenecks.

**Exit criterion:** The pipeline reliably produces a small number of high-quality decision conversations within Mindmake's delivery capacity.

## Quality-control checklist

### Research

- [ ] Target is a real person at the correct company.
- [ ] Role and decision authority are current.
- [ ] Trigger is current, material and sourced.
- [ ] Contrary evidence has been considered.
- [ ] Facts and hypotheses are visibly separated.
- [ ] Warm path is verified, not assumed from a connection count.

### Relevance

- [ ] One consequential decision is named.
- [ ] Mindmake is a fit for judgement/capability/GTM, not commodity delivery.
- [ ] The proposed proof can be bounded.
- [ ] The recipient has a reason to care now.
- [ ] The message would not survive a company-name swap.

### Relationship

- [ ] The ask matches relationship strength.
- [ ] Dormant relationships are handled honestly.
- [ ] Connector can decline without awkwardness.
- [ ] Introduction is double opt-in.
- [ ] Forwardable note is ready.
- [ ] Thank-you and loop closure are scheduled.

### Copy

- [ ] Opens with something relevant, not biography.
- [ ] Contains one thought and one ask.
- [ ] Avoids flattery and surveillance cues.
- [ ] Avoids fake urgency.
- [ ] Sounds like Krish, not a sales template.
- [ ] Keeps uncertainty honest.
- [ ] Gives value before asking for time.

### Proof

- [ ] Proof is permitted for this surface.
- [ ] Attribution level is preserved.
- [ ] Quote is exact if quoted.
- [ ] Attendance is not represented as a client relationship.
- [ ] Claim does not outrun evidence.
- [ ] No private price or identifier appears.

### Compliance

- [ ] Lawful basis is recorded.
- [ ] PECR category is understood.
- [ ] Privacy information is accessible.
- [ ] Suppression list has been checked.
- [ ] Sender identity and opt-out are clear.
- [ ] Data use is proportionate and unsurprising.
- [ ] Exact send has immediate Krish approval.

## Anti-patterns

Do not:

- Blast the network with an announcement asking for “anyone interested.”
- Lead with “I have launched a new business.”
- Make a connector work out who should buy.
- Ask for an introduction and then send a generic deck.
- Mention every Mindmake capability in one message.
- Over-personalise with irrelevant personal facts.
- Use AI to fabricate familiarity.
- Turn LinkedIn likes into permission to pitch.
- Confuse activity with a trigger.
- Diagnose a prospect's impostor syndrome.
- Promise transformation before naming the decision.
- Use public prices or unapproved claims.
- imply that big-brand attendees were consulting clients.
- Continue following up without new value.
- Hide an automated sequence behind faux-human language.
- Treat a name from a referrer as permission to contact.
- Forget to update the introducer.
- Automate sending because the draft passed checks.
- Build a large funnel that Mindmake cannot serve.

## The founder standard

Every outbound action should feel like this:

> Krish noticed something important, thought carefully about what it might mean, was honest about what he could not know from outside, offered a useful way to test the decision, and left the other person fully in control.

That is not merely a tone choice. It is the commercial model. Mindmake sells judgement that leaves the client stronger; its lead generation must demonstrate the same thing before a contract exists.
